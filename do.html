DO · ACTION

Do the next thing that honors God.

Put faith into action through repeated choices, habits, wise stewardship, Spirit-dependent prayer, and faithful community.

4 lessonsAbout 45 minutesIncludes the Time Audit

SELF-PACED LEARNING PATH

Turn truth into practice

Connect BE and KNOW to daily action, then choose one specific commitment you can begin this week.

  1. 01

    Let faith shape action

    Keep God’s truth—not experience, preference, or culture—as the starting point.

    +

    Learn

    The order matters: faith shapes values, values shape how you interpret experience, and those convictions guide daily action. Experiences and education matter, but they are not the final authority. God’s truth interprets your life rather than your life redefining God’s truth.

    Romans 8:28–30 places God’s work in context: He is forming His people to become more like Christ. Because Christ is victorious and God’s love is secure, you can make faithful decisions without being controlled by fear.

    Key Scripture

    Romans 8:28–38 ↗

    Reflect

    • Where are you tempted to reverse the order and begin with experience or preference?
    • What purpose is God working toward in His people?
    • How could confidence in Christ’s victory change a fear-driven decision?

    Key idea: God is forming you to become more like Christ; ordinary decisions become part of that transforming work.

  2. 02

    Build habits that reflect Christ

    Notice how decisions become repetitions, patterns, and habits that shape your life.

    +

    Learn

    Good or bad, repeated decisions become habits: decision → repetition → pattern → habit. Every habit forms you in a direction—either reinforcing who you are becoming in Christ or pulling you away from it. Busyness is not the same as faithfulness.

    Examine your patterns

    • Think of a habit that has had a positive impact. How did it become a habit?
    • Which habits reinforce your identity in Christ?
    • Which habits compete with it?
    • What is one small decision that becomes powerful when repeated?

    Key Scripture

    Romans 12:1–2; Galatians 6:7–10; 1 Timothy 4:7–8 ↗

    Practice

    Choose one habit to begin, strengthen, reduce, or stop. Define the next repetition: what will you do, when and where will it happen, and what obstacle should you prepare for?

    Ask: “What is this habit forming in me?”

  3. 03

    Audit your time and stewardship

    Compare what you say you value with how you actually use attention, energy, and time.

    +

    Learn

    Your calendar is one of the clearest records of lived priorities. The Time Audit is not a guilt exercise or a scorecard. It helps you stop guessing, look for repeated patterns, and make one faithful adjustment.

    Review the evidence

    • What surprised you about the way you use time?
    • Where does your schedule already reflect what God values?
    • Where is there a gap between stated and demonstrated values?
    • What might need to change first?

    Key Scripture

    Ephesians 5:15–17; Psalm 90:12 ↗

    Practice

    Complete the one-month Time Audit. Review it as evidence, looking for attention, energy, boundaries, competing priorities, and opportunities to realign.

    Key idea: Time is a God-given resource. Honest awareness can reveal one faithful adjustment.

  4. 04

    Prepare your toolbox

    Identify the people and resources you will use before the next difficult decision arrives.

    +

    Learn

    You are not meant to make difficult decisions alone. God gives His Spirit, Scripture, and faithful people. A useful toolbox is assembled before the problem arrives, so you already know which resources to use when pressure is high.

    Key Scripture

    Romans 8:26–27; Romans 12:2; 2 Timothy 3:14–17; Proverbs 15:22 ↗

    Inventory your resources

    • Church, Bible study, community group, and wise counsel
    • Prayer, quiet time, Scripture, and a biblical worldview
    • Your values statement, calendar, and financial budget
    • Service, fellowship, health, education, technology, and healthy hobbies

    Put faith in action

    • Who is one “Hebrews 11 person” who consistently points you toward God?
    • Which tool is strongest in your life? Which needs strengthening?
    • Return to the decision you named in BE. What is one next faithful step?

    Commitment: Complete the sentence, “This week, I will honor God by…”
